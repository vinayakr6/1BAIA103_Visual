UNDERSTAND: Components of intelligence

Reasoning

Using processes to make decisions and predictions; the material distinguishes inductive and deductive reasoning.

Learning

Improving behaviour or performance from experience or data.

Problem solving

Finding a sequence of actions or method that reaches a desired solution.

Perception

Acquiring, interpreting and selecting useful information from the environment.

Linguistic intelligence

Ability to use, comprehend, speak and write verbal and written language.

INTERACT: Inductive vs Deductive reasoning

InductiveDeductive
Moves from observations/examples toward a generalisation.Moves from a general rule/premise toward a specific conclusion.
Conclusion is supported by observed patterns.Conclusion follows from accepted premises when the reasoning is valid.
Example: several observed birds fly → infer a pattern.Rule: all members of a defined class have property X; object is a member → infer X.

Human vs Machine Intelligence

Human intelligenceMachine intelligence
Flexible, embodied and influenced by rich experience and context.Often highly effective within defined objectives and representations.
Can combine social, emotional and common-sense understanding.Can process large datasets rapidly and consistently for programmed/learned tasks.
Learning can occur from relatively few examples.Many AI methods depend strongly on data, representations and training.
