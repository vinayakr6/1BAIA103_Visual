2 · What is Artificial Intelligence?

AI = Making machines capable of intelligent behaviour

The course text presents AI as the science and engineering of making intelligent machines, especially intelligent computer programs. It also describes AI from a practical viewpoint as machine intelligence that can mimic aspects of human intelligence, learn from experience, adjust to new inputs and perform tasks that normally require human intelligence.

Core idea: AI is not simply “a computer doing a calculation.” The important idea is that a system can use information to recognize patterns, learn, reason, perceive, make decisions or solve problems.

Five useful ways to think about intelligence in AI

👁️
Perceive

Take information from the environment, such as an image, sound, text or sensor reading.

📚
Learn

Use data or experience to identify patterns and improve performance.

🧠
Reason

Select an appropriate method or rule to reach a desired outcome.

🎯
Decide

Choose an action, prediction or response based on available information.

4 · How AI Works

Three cognitive skills: Learning → Reasoning → Self-correction

📚
1. Learning

Acquire data and create rules or patterns for turning data into actionable information.

🧠
2. Reasoning

Use an appropriate algorithm or learned representation to reach the desired outcome.

⚙️
3. Action

Generate a prediction, classification, recommendation, answer or control action.

🔍
4. Evaluate

Compare the output with the task objective or observed result.

🔁
5. Self-correct

Improve the algorithm/model so later outputs can become more accurate.

Important: “Learning” does not mean every AI system learns continuously while you use it. The course resource describes learning, reasoning and self-correction as key cognitive skills of AI programming; the actual learning process depends on the system design.
5 · Detailed Example

How could an AI chatbot work?

Step 1 — Examples

The system receives large amounts of training information such as text examples.

Step 2 — Pattern discovery

Algorithms analyse the data and identify useful statistical or structural patterns.

Step 3 — Model

The learned patterns are represented in a computational model.

Step 4 — User input

A user enters a question or instruction.

Step 5 — Output

The system generates a response based on its learned representation and processing method.

Step 6 — Evaluation

The quality of responses can be evaluated and the system can be improved through suitable training or engineering.

7 · Traditional Programming vs AI/ML

What changes when the system learns from data?

Traditional programmingMachine-learning-oriented approach
Programmer explicitly writes rules/logic.Algorithm learns patterns from data.
Input + fixed rules → output.Data + learning method → model; model + new input → prediction.
Rules remain fixed until code is changed.A trained model can be updated/retrained as data changes.
Useful when rules are clear and deterministic.Useful when patterns are difficult to express as hand-written rules.
Do not oversimplify: AI is not always machine learning. AI includes multiple approaches. ML is one important branch of AI, and deep learning is a further subset of ML.
8 · Why AI is Important

What makes AI useful?

⚡ Scale

AI can process very large volumes of data quickly.

🎯 Consistency

AI applications can produce consistent results when the same conditions and model are used.

🕐 Availability

AI-based systems can be designed to operate continuously.

🤖 Automation

AI can automate complex or repetitive processes.

🔮 Prediction

AI/ML can identify patterns useful for prediction and decision support.

🛠️ Optimization

AI can help utilize resources more effectively and support maintenance prediction.

12 · Misconceptions

Stop-and-think

“AI is the same as any computer program.”

Not every computer program is AI. The course frames AI around capabilities associated with intelligent behaviour such as learning, reasoning, perception, problem solving and decision-making.

“AI always learns by itself after deployment.”

Not necessarily. A system may be trained beforehand and then deployed for inference. Whether it learns after deployment depends on its design.

“More data automatically means better AI.”

Data quality matters. The course specifically notes that incomplete or inaccurate data can produce disastrous results.

“AI can do everything humans can do.”

The module distinguishes specialized AI capabilities from broad human intelligence. Many current AI systems are designed for particular tasks.

16 · Takeaways
  • AI aims to enable machines to perform tasks associated with intelligence.
  • AI systems can involve perception, learning, reasoning, decision-making and problem solving.
  • A useful conceptual workflow is data/input → analysis → model/algorithm → prediction/decision → evaluation/feedback.
  • The course emphasizes learning, reasoning and self-correction.
  • AI has significant advantages, but cost, expertise, data quality and generalization are important limitations.
  • Machine Learning is a branch of AI; Deep Learning is a subset of Machine Learning.
